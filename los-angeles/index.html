Keeping Your Los Angeles Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Well before a tank floods a Los Angeles garage, it usually shows warning signs. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
