Keeping Your Hawthorne Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Hawthorne home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
